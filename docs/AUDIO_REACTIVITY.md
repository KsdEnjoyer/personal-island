# Audio-reactive lower edge

The old time-only sine waves are replaced by a local WASAPI render-loopback analyser in `audio_reactivity.rs`. Ten constant-Q bands cover bass through treble. Stereo channels are analysed separately, preserving out-of-phase bass. A slow loudness reference and transient detector make sudden bass entries more prominent than steady passages; silence has a noise gate.

The frontend receives only ten band levels, bass, level, impact and availability, at up to 30 events/second. PCM is processed in memory, never written to disk or sent to the web/MCP. The source is the default Windows multimedia output mix; other audio playing on that output can contribute. Microphone input is never opened. Changing the default output reconnects the stream.

The filled island body has a fixed rounded lower boundary. A separate stroked waveform runs along that boundary: frequency levels change its peaks, while a bass transient briefly increases their amplitude. Audio never changes the filled silhouette, its shadow, the controls or the hitbox. The waveform has a 4 ms attack and 45–80 ms release, with stronger bass near the centre and finer high-frequency detail toward the shoulders. A power curve brings out ordinary passages; transient gain adds a short, stronger punch. Spatial phase follows measured energy rather than a clock-driven music animation. Soft compression limits the amplitude envelope before shaping the waveform, preserving curved crests instead of hard-clipping samples into a flat ceiling. Its centre shifts outward with energy: inward peaks stay within 4 logical pixels and outward peaks can reach about 34 logical pixels. A subtle stroke halo follows transients. Only opening/closing the dashboard changes body dimensions. Missing/stale frames fade away. Reduced motion or disabling the liquid edge stops analysis. Hiding or pausing also stops analysis, and the native visibility check independently releases loopback even if a frontend enable is stale. Hidden canvas rendering stops after transitions settle.

Validation: Rust signal tests cover silence, a bass drop, high frequencies, stereo phase and PCM formats. `qa-audio-reactivity-ui.py` separately checks waveform pixels and the opaque filled-body boundary across quiet/drop frames, plus attack/release and lifecycle gating. `qa-audio-native.py` exercises real Windows loopback with a short, modest-level 75 Hz fixture in an isolated app/profile/database; it does not change system/player volume settings.

API references: [WASAPI loopback](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/loopback-recording), [capture buffers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/audioclient/nf-audioclient-iaudiocaptureclient-getbuffer).
